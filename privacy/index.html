Privacy Policy

Applies to Hush version 1.0 and later. Last updated 20 September 2026.

The short version

Hush collects nothing about you. It cannot see who calls you, and it is never told that a call happened.

That is not a promise about how we intend to behave. It is a description of how iPhone call blocking works, and this page explains the difference between the parts that could never change and the parts that describe the app as it stands today.

Two kinds of statement on this page

Most privacy policies mix these together. We have separated them, because one kind is worth far more than the other and you deserve to know which is which.

Guarantees are true because of the way Apple built call blocking, not because we chose to be careful. Breaking one would mean abandoning the mechanism the app is built on. They carry no date, because they do not expire.

Facts about this version describe the app released today. They are accurate and they are dated. A future version could change one, and if that ever happens it will appear in the change history at the bottom of this page rather than being quietly edited away.

Guarantees

Hush is never told that a call happened

Hush uses Apple's Call Directory system. The app hands iOS a list of phone numbers in advance. When a call arrives, iOS checks that list itself. Hush is not running, is not consulted, and receives nothing.

This is why Hush cannot show you a count of blocked calls. Some people expect that feature. It cannot exist without the app seeing your calls, so it does not exist here, and it will not exist later.

Hush cannot see who is calling you

It follows from the above. The app never receives a caller's number, so there is nothing for it to store, count, or send anywhere. This is not a setting you have to trust us to leave switched off. There is no switch.

Nothing on the internet decides whether your call is blocked

The decision is made on your iPhone, by iOS, against a list already on the device. Blocking works with no signal, in aeroplane mode, with no SIM, and on a network you have never used before.

Hush never asks for your contacts

Not as a requirement, and not as an optional extra we nag you about. The app has no code that reads them.

There is no account

You never create one, and you are never asked for a name, an email address, or a phone number. We have no way to identify you and no way to link a subscription to a person.

What is stored on your iPhone

A handful of small settings, none of which describes you: whether you have finished the introduction, a version number for the settings themselves so a future update knows how to read them, which version of this policy your iPhone has seen, the last answer the App Store gave about your subscription and when, which range of numbers iOS was last given and when, and whether the reminders described below have been scheduled. One of them, whether the subscription is active, is shared with the part of Hush that iOS runs, and nothing else can read it. All of them stay on your device, and none of them says who you are.

The list of blocked numbers is not stored. It is calculated on your iPhone when protection is switched on.

Internet and servers

This version of Hush contains no networking code of its own. There is no server holding your data, because there is no company system for your data to reach.

The only network activity associated with Hush is Apple's own, when you buy or restore a subscription. That is handled entirely by the App Store. We never see your payment details, and Apple's privacy policy governs it.

If that ever changes

A future version may download an updated list of which number ranges to block, so that coverage can keep up with the people sending the calls. If it does:

  • It would be a one way download of a public file. Nothing about you or your phone would be sent.
  • It would be checked cryptographically before being trusted, and a list that failed the check would be discarded rather than used.
  • It could not affect the guarantees above. The decision about an incoming call would still be made on your device, offline, with the app never told what the number was.
  • It would appear in the change history below, before the version shipped.

We are telling you this in advance rather than presenting it as a surprise later. Anything beyond it, in particular an account, a report a number feature, or anything that sends us information about your calls, would be a different product and would require your consent, not a policy edit.

Notifications

Hush can send you up to three reminders if you have subscribed but have not yet switched Hush on under Call Blocking and Identification. Without that switch, Hush blocks nothing, and we would rather tell you than let your trial end on a blocker that never blocked anything.

You are asked for permission once, straight after you subscribe, and you can say no. The reminders stop the moment you switch Hush on. They are only ever sent to someone who has paid, never as advertising.

These are made by your iPhone, from your iPhone's own clock. Hush has no server, registers for no push notifications, and could not send you one if it wanted to. The wording is fixed in the app and is the same for everybody, so nothing about you is involved in it.

Subscriptions

Subscriptions are processed by Apple through the App Store. Hush receives only which plan is active on the device and when it ends, which is how it knows when to stop. It does not receive your name, your email address, your payment method, or any Apple Account information.

Children

Hush does not collect data from anyone, including children.

How this policy changes

The date at the top changes with any edit. Every substantive change is listed in the change history below, with what changed and when. A change that reduced your privacy would require an app update, which you would have to choose to install.

We will not remove entries from the change history.
